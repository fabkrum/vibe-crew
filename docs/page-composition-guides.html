Page Composition Guides

Research-backed composition guides for 12 common page types. Each guide describes the essential zones a page needs, which existing patterns to use, common mistakes to avoid, and structural variations to choose from. These are composition guides, not rigid templates — they describe required zones and reference existing patterns without dictating layout, style, or ordering.

Conversion Pages

Pages designed to turn visitors into customers — landing pages, pricing, and product details that maximize conversion through research-backed zone composition.

Landing Page

Convert visitor attention into a single, specific action by presenting a focused value proposition, removing decision friction, and building trust before the page ends.

NNGroup, Baymard Institute, CXL Institute, Unbounce 2024 Benchmark

Research

NNGroup eye-tracking across 57,453 fixations shows users spend 57% of page-viewing time above the fold. Landing pages with social proof convert 34% better than those without (Unbounce 2024, 41,000+ pages). Baymard Institute finds 21% of users abandon due to unanswered questions about security, pricing, or cancellation.

Common Mistakes
MistakeFix
Headline leads with a feature ("Advanced analytics dashboard") instead of an outcome Rewrite as the transformation: "Know exactly why users drop off, in under a minute." Features belong in the feature showcase, not the hero.
Social proof placed only in the footer where high-intent visitors never see it Place a logo rail below the hero and at least one attributed testimonial within 300px of the primary CTA.
Full site navigation with dropdowns gives visitors 12 escape routes before they understand the value Strip nav to logo + single ghost CTA. Conversion pages are not discovery pages.
Wall of text with no visual hierarchy forces everyone to read everything before orienting Alternate full-width narrative and grid layouts. Use section headers that are scannable standalone sentences.
Variations
Short-Form Focused

For high-intent traffic (paid search, retargeting) with low-commitment asks — hero, CTA, logo rail, one social proof element.

Long-Form Narrative

For complex or expensive products — walk through problem, solution, features, proof, pricing, FAQ, and final CTA using scroll-triggered animations.

Problem-Agitate-Solve (PAS)

Open with the problem, agitate with consequences, introduce the product as the fix — used by Basecamp and early Shopify.

Pricing Page

Convert evaluation-stage prospects into paying customers by resolving value uncertainty, pre-empting purchase objections, and guiding visitors toward the plan that best fits their use case.

CXL Institute, Dan Ariely, Baymard Institute, NNGroup
Essential Zones
  • Required Value Hook Header

    Anchors context — confirms the visitor is on the right page and restates the core value proposition.

    Placement Above the fold, before plan cards

Research

CXL Institute A/B testing shows highlighting the recommended plan with a "Most Popular" badge increases selection by 25–40%. Dan Ariely's decoy research demonstrates adding a third tier increases midrange selection by up to 85%. Baymard finds 21% abandon when key questions go unanswered; a visible 30-day guarantee lifts paid conversions by 21%.

Common Mistakes
MistakeFix
More than four plan tiers creating decision paralysis Limit to three named tiers (Good/Better/Best) plus one enterprise contact row. Hick's Law and Baymard studies confirm 4+ options increase abandonment.
Hiding prices behind "Contact Sales" for all tiers, even self-serve segments Show transparent pricing for all self-serve plans. Reserve "Contact Sales" for the enterprise tier where procurement complexity justifies it.
Feature comparison table buried with no anchor link from plan cards Add a visible "Compare all features" link inside each plan card that deep-links to the comparison table.
Trust signals and security badges placed only in the site footer, far from CTAs Place at least one trust signal within one viewport of each plan card's CTA button.
Variations
Card-First (Self-Serve SaaS)

Lead with plan cards above the fold — no hero preamble. For high-intent branded traffic (Linear, Vercel, GitHub).

Value-First (Horizontal Funnel)

Open with benefit headline and trust bar before plan cards. For pricing pages that also serve cold search traffic (Notion, Shopify).

Segment-Gated (Persona Routing)

Show Individual/Team/Enterprise selector before revealing plans. For products with significantly different feature sets per segment (Stripe, Salesforce).

Product / Service Detail

Give visitors the precise information, trust signals, and decision-making context to confidently add to cart, start a trial, or contact sales — without seeking information elsewhere.

Baymard Institute, NNGroup, CXL Institute, Spiegel Research Center
Essential Zones
  • Required Product Hero / Buy Box

    Establishes what the product is, what it costs, and what the primary action is — above the fold.

    Placement Top of page. Desktop: image gallery left, buy box right. Mobile: image stack top, buy box below.

    Placement Left column on desktop, full-width carousel on mobile. Thumbnails always visible.

    Placement Below buy box on desktop, below media on mobile

Research

Baymard Institute's 236-subject study found 82% of e-commerce sites have mediocre-to-poor product page UX. Products with 50+ reviews convert at 4.6x the rate of those with none (Spiegel Research). 56% of users' first action on a product page is exploring the image gallery.

Common Mistakes
MistakeFix
Using horizontal tabs to organize specs, FAQs, and reviews Replace with vertically collapsed accordions or a sticky TOC long page. 27% of users overlook horizontal tab content (Baymard).
Trust signals (guarantee, return policy, secure checkout) placed only in footer Move trust signals into or directly below the buy box, within the same visual container as the CTA button.
Truncating the image gallery to 1–2 images with no visible thumbnails Always show thumbnail navigation. Truncating causes 50–80% of users to overlook additional images.
Cross-sell carousels placed before or within the primary buy zone Place all cross-sells after reviews and FAQ. The primary conversion path must be unobstructed.
Variations
Buy-Box Split Layout

Two-column desktop layout (media left, buy box right) for physical or visible-deliverable products — the highest-performing layout per Baymard benchmarks.

Hero + Feature Scroll (SaaS)

Single-column long page with hero screenshot, social proof, alternating feature sections, pricing, FAQ — for software products (Stripe, Linear, Vercel).

Long Page with Sticky TOC

Full-width sections with persistent table-of-contents navigation for complex/technical products where users jump between specs and comparisons (Apple Mac Pro).

Content Pages

Pages that deliver long-form content while building authority, engagement, and subscriber conversion.

Blog / Article

Deliver long-form content to readers with specific information needs while establishing authority, maximizing engagement, and converting engaged readers into subscribers or leads.

NNGroup, Simpleview, HubSpot, CXL Institute
Essential Zones
  • Required Article Header

    Establishes the topic, author credibility, and time investment before the reader commits to scrolling.

    Placement Full-width top of page, above body text

    Heading HierarchyOpen Graph TagsArticle SchemaAuthority SignalsResponsive Images
  • Required Reading Context Strip

    Sets reader expectations with category, reading time, and publish date — reduces bounce from uncertain visitors.

    Placement Below headline, before body copy. Single horizontal row.

    Article SchemaBreadcrumbList SchemaProgress Indicators
  • Required Article Body

    Delivers the content in a scannable, comprehension-optimized format respecting F-shaped and layer-cake scanning patterns.

    Placement Center column, 60–75ch line width

    Heading HierarchyF-Pattern ScanningImage Gallery Grid
  • Required Author Attribution

    Provides E-E-A-T signals (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness) required by both readers and Google quality raters.

    Placement After article body, before related content

    Authority SignalsArticle Schema
  • Required In-Article Conversion CTA

    Captures highest-intent readers at peak relevance. Inline contextual CTAs convert 121% higher than banner CTAs (HubSpot).

    Placement End of article (primary), optionally mid-article at ~50–60% scroll depth

    CTA HierarchyFormFade
Recommended Zones
  • Optional Sticky Table of Contents

    Gives readers a persistent mental map and allows non-linear navigation through long articles.

    Placement Left sidebar on wide viewports, collapsible block on mobile

    Heading HierarchyProgress IndicatorsScroll Reveal
  • Optional Social Sharing Bar

    Reduces friction for readers who want to share by surfacing platform-specific share actions.

    Placement Floating left sidebar on wide viewports, static block at top/bottom on narrow

    Open Graph TagsCanonical URL Tag
  • Optional Related Articles

    Reduces exit rate and deepens topic authority by routing engaged readers to semantically related content.

    Placement Below author block and primary CTA, before footer

    Internal Linking StrategyScroll RevealResponsive Images
  • Optional Reading Progress Indicator

    Provides continuous visual orientation for long-form articles, encouraging completion over abandonment.

    Placement Thin progress bar fixed to top of viewport

    Progress Indicators
Research

NNGroup eyetracking finds content above the fold receives 57% of viewing time, each subsequent screen getting ~33% less attention. Adding estimated reading time increases engagement by up to 40% (Simpleview) and time on site by 13.8%. Layer-cake scanning means subheadings determine whether body content gets read at all.

Common Mistakes
MistakeFix
No author identity — publishing under "Admin" or with no byline removes all E-E-A-T signals Every article needs a named author with photo, role, and link to an author page. Include Person schema in Article structured data.
Wall of text with no subheadings, pull quotes, or whitespace H2/H3 every 200–300 words, opening sentences that stand alone as scannable summaries, bullets for lists, 65–75ch max paragraph width.
Newsletter CTA placed only in sidebar or at very bottom of page Use two placements: one inline CTA after section 2–3, and one end-of-article CTA. Limit to 1–2 total to avoid overload.
Missing Article structured data (no datePublished, dateModified, or author Person) Implement full Article JSON-LD with headline, image, dates, author (Person + sameAs), publisher, and wordCount.
Variations
Distraction-Free Reading

Removes sidebar nav, collapses header on scroll, centers text in a narrow column — for publications where completion rate is the metric (Medium, Substack).

Technical / Developer Blog

Prioritizes syntax-highlighted code blocks, persistent sticky TOC, wide code column, and "copy to clipboard" — for audiences scanning for implementation details (Vercel Blog, Stripe Blog).

Commercial Content Marketing

Multiple inline CTAs for gated assets, right sidebar offers, sticky bottom bar CTA — for high-traffic informational articles where lead generation is the goal (HubSpot, CXL).

Trust & Company Pages

Pages that establish organizational credibility, humanize the team, and facilitate direct communication.

About / Team

Convert skeptical evaluators into confident buyers by establishing organizational credibility, humanizing the people behind the product, and proving the company can be trusted.

NNGroup, CXL Institute, Sprout Social, HubSpot
Essential Zones
  • Required Company Story

    Answers "why does this company exist?" and creates an emotional hook that builds rapid trust through shared values.

    Placement First section below the hero statement

    Authority SignalsHeading HierarchyOrganization SchemaScroll Reveal
  • Required Mission & Values

    Lets evaluators self-select — visitors who align with stated values self-qualify as better-fit customers.

    Placement Below or integrated into the company story

    Authority SignalsOrganization SchemaStaggered List
  • Required Traction Stats

    Establishes proof-of-scale with specific, credible numbers that answer the implicit "are you for real?" question.

    Placement Horizontal strip below hero/mission or as a divider between story and team

    Social Proof PlacementAuthority SignalsOrganization Schema
  • Required Team Grid

    Humanizes the organization — faces reduce perceived risk of dealing with an abstract entity.

    Placement Below traction metrics. Small teams: show everyone. Large: founders + leadership.

    Authority SignalsStaggered ListResponsive Images
  • Required Backer / Partner Logos

    Third-party institutional validation borrows credibility at zero cost — someone credible already vetted this company.

    Placement Compact logo row below team or in traction area

    Social Proof PlacementTrust Signal Placement
  • Required Closing CTA

    Captures intent from high-intent evaluators. About page visitors are verifying credibility, not browsing.

    Placement Final section, below all trust content

    CTA HierarchyFormFade
Recommended Zones
  • Optional Press Coverage ("As Seen In")

    Earned media logos signal newsworthiness and third-party validation without the company saying anything about itself.

    Placement Horizontal strip above or below traction metrics

    Social Proof PlacementAuthority Signals
  • Optional Company Timeline

    Demonstrates longevity, momentum, and shipping history — countering "will this company still exist next year?"

    Placement Within or below the company story section

    Authority SignalsScroll Reveal
  • Optional Careers Teaser

    Signals active growth while capturing talent interest. A growing team is itself a trust signal.

    Placement Near-bottom, before or after closing CTA

    Social Proof PlacementCTA Hierarchy
Research

NNGroup finds About page visitors are in credibility-verification mode — they leave quickly if questions go unanswered. CXL research shows trust signals near CTAs increase conversions by 11–42%. Sprout Social data shows 57% of consumers spend more with brands they feel connected to, and 76% choose them over competitors.

Common Mistakes
MistakeFix
Generic jargon mission statement ("We're passionate about empowering teams to reach their potential") Replace with concrete specifics — name the problem, the user, and what the company does differently. NNGroup shows users perceive vague language as evasion.
Stock photography or overly staged team photos that don't show real people Use authentic headshots with consistent lighting and background. The fusiform face area triggers automatic attention to faces — fake ones signal inauthenticity.
No CTA at the end — treating the About page as a dead end Place a contextual CTA at the close (start trial, contact sales, or view careers). About visitors are high-intent evaluators.
About page buried in footer navigation, hard to find from primary nav Make accessible from primary navigation. It's typically a top-5 visited page on B2B/SaaS sites (96% of prospects self-research per HubSpot).
Variations
Founder-Led / Early-Stage

Lead with founder story and personal photo. Team is a single founder bio. Focus on why-I-built-this narrative when the founder's credibility is the primary trust asset (<15 people).

Institution / Scale-First

Open with traction stats and customer logos before narrative, then mission and large team grid — for 100+ customer companies where institutional proof outweighs origin story (Linear, Stripe, Notion).

Mission-Centered / Community

Structure around a singular cause or POV with diversity of thought and a values manifesto — for developer tools, open-source, or philosophy-differentiated companies (GitLab, Vercel).

Contact

Convert visitor intent into a direct communication channel by providing multiple contact methods, routing to the correct team, and building trust that makes someone willing to reach out.

NNGroup, Baymard Institute, CXL Institute, Smashing Magazine
Essential Zones
  • Required Channel Selector

    Routes users to the correct team (Sales, Support, Partnerships) before they reach a form, reducing abandonment and misroutes.

    Placement Top of page, above the fold — never make users scroll to find how to contact you

    CTA HierarchyFade
  • Required Contact Form

    Captures structured inquiry data and routes it to the right team with validated, actionable information.

    Placement Center of page, below channel selector or conditionally shown after intent selection

    FormInline ValidationSingle-Column LayoutRequired/Optional MarkingAutocomplete TokensShimmer Loading
  • Required Direct Contact Details

    Provides phone, email, and address for users who distrust forms or whose needs are urgent.

    Placement Sidebar alongside form, or directly above it — never below the fold

    Authority SignalsTrust Signal PlacementOrganization Schema
  • Required Response Time Expectation

    Eliminates uncertainty that causes abandonment by stating exactly when and how someone will respond.

    Placement Below or adjacent to the submit button

    Trust Signal PlacementInline Validation
Recommended Zones
  • Optional FAQ / Self-Serve Deflection

    Resolves common queries before submission, reducing support volume while improving user confidence.

    Placement Below form or in sidebar

    FAQ StructureFAQ SchemaCollapse
  • Optional Office Location & Map

    Provides physical legitimacy and supports local SEO for businesses with walk-in locations.

    Placement Lower section, below primary contact form

    Authority SignalsOrganization Schema
  • Optional Team Presence Signal

    Humanizes the contact experience by showing who will actually respond, reducing fear of a void.

    Placement Alongside the form

    Authority SignalsResponsive Images
  • Optional Meeting Scheduler

    Lets high-intent visitors book a call immediately without waiting for email response.

    Placement Alternative tab or secondary section when "Talk to Sales" is selected

    CTA HierarchyMulti-Step Form
Research

NNGroup research across 40 sites found users actively distrust pages with only a form and no phone number — "suspicious" was the word used. Baymard Institute found sites with full validation achieve 78% one-try submissions vs 42% without. CXL shows reducing a form from 11 to 4 fields produces 160% more submissions.

Common Mistakes
MistakeFix
Form-only page with no phone number, email, or physical address Always display direct contact details alongside the form. Removing phone numbers erodes trust (NNGroup). The form supplements real contact, not replaces it.
Not setting response time expectations — users submit and hear nothing for days State a specific SLA below the submit button ("We respond within 1 business day") and repeat in the confirmation message.
Requiring account creation before allowing contact Contact access must be unconditional. Account walls on contact pages are a highest-friction failure pattern (NNGroup).
Routing all inquiry types to a single generic inbox via one undifferentiated form Implement a channel selector or intent router at the top (Sales / Support / Press). Card-based routing directs to the appropriate team and form variant.
Variations
Single-Form with Channel Dropdown

One form with a "What can we help with?" dropdown that conditionally reveals fields — best for small teams or low contact volume.

Intent Card Router (Multi-Path)

Grid of labeled cards (Sales, Support, Press) each linking to a distinct form — best for B2B SaaS with distinct personas and routing (Calendly, Stripe, HubSpot).

Embedded Scheduler with Qualification

Minimal 3-field form that reveals a calendar embed on completion — best for sales pages where the goal is booking demos (reduces time-to-meeting from days to seconds).
